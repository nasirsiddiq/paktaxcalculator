Zakat Calculator

Estimate your zakatable wealth, track your Hawl (Islamic year), and calculate Zakat due — in your own currency.

Check Your Zakat Eligibility

Based on the Shariah conditions for Zakat becoming obligatory (Hanafi fiqh), as compiled in Fatawa-e-Ahlesunnat: Ahkam-e-Zakat. Answer these questions to see whether Zakat is due on you this year.

1. Are you Muslim, of sound mind (ʿĀqil), and have you reached adulthood / puberty (Bāligh)?

2. Do you have full, exclusive ownership of this wealth — not something held in trust for someone else, or not yet in your possession?

3. Is this wealth of a "growing" nature — cash, gold/silver, trade goods, or investments — rather than personal-use items like your home, car, or furniture?

4. Does your wealth reach Nisab? Zakat becomes due if any one of these four is true:

  1. You hold gold alone reaching its own Nisab — 87.48g (7.5 tola)
  2. You hold silver alone reaching its own Nisab — 612.36g
  3. You hold cash/bank balance alone worth the value of 612.36g of silver, at today's price
  4. You hold a mix of gold, silver and/or cash — none reaching its own threshold alone — but their combined value still reaches the value of 612.36g of silver

Enter what you actually hold below (after subtracting debts and basic living needs) and we'll check which condition, if any, applies — amounts shown in PKR.

e.g. loan/installments due, committee (Bisi) balance owed, unpaid utility bills, dealer payables, unpaid salaries, last year's unpaid Zakat

Gold, silver and exchange rates always come from a single lookup for the same date, so they never drift apart. Already fetched rates below? This reuses them — no need to fetch twice.

5. Has a full Islamic (Hijri) year — a Hawl — passed since your wealth first reached Nisab, staying at or above it throughout?

Source: conditions of Zakat obligation (Shurut al-Wujub) as cited in Fatawa-e-Ahlesunnat: Ahkam-e-Zakat, quoting Tanwir al-Absar: "The conditions of its obligation are: sanity, puberty, Islam, freedom, ownership of Nisab for a full year — free of debt and basic necessities — and the wealth being of a growing nature." A separate historical condition of personal freedom (not being enslaved) is universally met today and isn't asked above.

This uses the silver standard for Nisab (the more precautionary threshold most scholars use), except when gold is your only zakatable asset — then Nisab is assessed by gold's own weight (7.5 tola / 87.48g), not its market value, per the Hanafi position. As soon as any other zakatable wealth is added, everything is combined and measured against the silver-value standard again. Zakat is calculated on the current value of your wealth at the time you calculate/pay it — not its value on the day you first reached Nisab.

Who Can Receive Your Zakat

Zakat must go to one of these categories, per the Holy Quran (Surah At-Taubah, 9:60):

  • The poor (Fuqara)
  • The destitute (Masakeen)
  • Zakat collectors/administrators
  • Those in debt (Al-Gharimin)
  • In the cause of Allah (Fi Sabilillah)
  • Stranded travelers (Ibn as-Sabil)

It cannot be given to:

  • Your own parents, grandparents, children, grandchildren, or spouse — you're already responsible for their upkeep
  • Sadaat/Bani Hashim (descendants of the Prophet's family), per the Hanafi position
  • A non-Muslim, or someone who already owns wealth at or above Nisab — this includes surplus non-essential items (e.g. extra furniture or valuables beyond everyday need), not just cash, gold and silver

Madani Pearl: if you yourself don't qualify as a Zakat recipient, your Zakat can still be given to your adult children or spouse if they independently meet the eligibility criteria above (e.g. an adult child who is poor and owns no Nisab of their own).

Sources: eligible categories per Quran 9:60 (a historical 7th category, for freeing slaves, no longer applies today); exclusions and the Madani Pearl per Dawat-e-Islami's own Zakat calculator (events.dawateislami.net) and the Hanafi position cited in Fatawa-e-Ahlesunnat: Ahkam-e-Zakat.

Live and historical exchange, gold and silver rates are fetched from a third-party public API (frankfurter.dev) at the moment you click a "Fetch Rates" button. They are provided for convenience and may occasionally be unavailable or slightly delayed — always spot-check against another source before paying a large amount of Zakat. This tool gives a general estimate for guidance only and is not a religious ruling (fatwa). Rules on jewelry, business valuation, and specific asset classes vary between schools of thought — please consult a knowledgeable scholar or your local imam for your specific situation.
