Ex-FBR Chief Shabbar Zaidi Hit With Rs1 Billion Defamation Notice From Jamaat-e-Islami

Published August 13, 2026 · paktaxcalculator.net

Jamaat-e-Islami (JI) has sent a Rs1 billion legal notice to Syed Shabbar Zaidi, Pakistan's former Federal Board of Revenue chairman, over remarks he made describing the party as the “establishment's firewall” for diluting public protests. The notice, dated July 27, 2026, demands Zaidi produce evidence for the claim or face a defamation suit.

What Happened

According to reporting by Dawn, JI's Karachi chapter — acting through advocate Tahir Iqbal Malik on the instructions of General Secretary Taufiquddin Siddiqui — served the notice after Zaidi made comments during a podcast interview and then repeated them across social media, including YouTube, Facebook, X, TikTok and WhatsApp. In those remarks, Zaidi described JI as functioning as an “establishment's firewall” used to dilute public issues and protests.

The Allegations JI Says Are Implied

JI's lawyer argues that Zaidi's characterization amounts to a set of specific accusations against the party, not just a general political jab. In the notice, these are described as claims of:

  • Secret funding
  • Bribery
  • Corruption
  • Political deception
  • Betrayal of public interests
  • Alleged collusion with state elements

JI is treating the podcast remarks and subsequent social media posts as a single, escalating public claim rather than an offhand comment — which is the basis for the Rs1 billion damages figure in the notice.

What JI Is Demanding

Rather than filing a defamation suit outright, the notice first demands that Zaidi substantiate his claims with concrete evidence, including:

  • The identity of the alleged payer and recipient
  • The payment amount, date and location
  • The method of payment and any bank records
  • Which specific protests were allegedly affected
  • Witnesses and supporting documentation

This is a common structure for a pre-litigation defamation notice in Pakistan: it puts the burden on Zaidi to either back up the claim or retract it before JI proceeds to an actual Rs1 billion damages suit.

Zaidi's Response

When Dawn contacted Zaidi directly, he confirmed receipt of the notice and said he had forwarded it to his lawyer for legal consultation. He has not, as of this writing, issued a public statement defending the substance of his original claim or responding point-by-point to JI's demand for evidence.

Not Zaidi's Only Legal Matter This Year

This defamation notice is separate from an earlier, unrelated case: in late 2025, the Federal Investigation Agency's Anti-Corruption Circle in Karachi registered an FIR against Zaidi alleging that unauthorised tax refunds of roughly Rs16 billion were made to several companies — reportedly including Habib Bank, Engro and DG Khan Cement — during his time as FBR chairman, with FBR and bank officials accused of misusing their authority. That case concerns his conduct in office and has no reported connection to the JI defamation dispute, which is about comments Zaidi made publicly after leaving FBR.

Who Is Shabbar Zaidi

Syed Mohammad Shabbar Zaidi is a chartered accountant who spent over four decades with A.F. Ferguson & Co. (a member firm of PwC) before serving as the 26th Chairman of FBR from May 9, 2019 to April 6, 2020. During his tenure he introduced the Asset Declaration Scheme, which the government credited with “whitening” roughly Rs3 trillion in previously undeclared assets and generating about Rs70 billion in revenue. He was removed from the post in April 2020 and succeeded by Nausheen Javed Amjad. Zaidi has since remained a prominent economic commentator, television analyst and author on Pakistani tax and fiscal policy.
