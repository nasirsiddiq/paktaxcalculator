Federal GST on goods and provincial sales tax on services are levied on different supplies, so Separate amounts is the accurate choice for a mixed invoice: enter the goods portion and the services portion, and each tax is applied only to its own portion. One amount for both applies both taxes to the same figure, which suits a single value you want tested against both levies but will overstate the tax on a genuinely mixed invoice. Each tax is worked out under the general withholding rule using its own supplier status — an agent deducts 1/5th (20%) of the tax from a registered, ATL-active supplier, and the whole (100%) applicable tax from an unregistered one. Because an unregistered supplier issues no tax invoice showing tax separately, an amount whose supplier is unregistered is treated as already including that tax, which is extracted with the tax fraction rate/(100+rate); tax for a registered supplier is added on top instead. Several categories depart from the 20%/100% rule (advertisement services withheld in full regardless of registration, federal wholesalers/dealers at 1/10th, Punjab telecom/banking/insurance at 80%, and others) — for those, use the detailed Sales Tax Withholding calculator, which models each category separately. Income tax withholding under s.153 is deducted from the same payment but is not included here — see the Withholding Tax (WHT) calculator. Rates change frequently; confirm with FBR or the relevant provincial authority before relying on these figures for filing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does this invoice have two different sales taxes on it?

Goods and services are taxed under separate laws in Pakistan: goods fall under the federal Sales Tax Act, 1990 (GST), while services fall under whichever provincial or territorial sales tax law applies to where the service is rendered (PRA, SRB, KPRA, BRA, or ICT). A single invoice covering both a product and a service genuinely owes both taxes, each on its own portion.

When should I use "Separate amounts" instead of "One amount for both"?

Use "Separate amounts" whenever you can split the invoice into a goods value and a services value — it's the accurate calculation. "One amount for both" is only useful as a quick check of what each tax would be if the whole amount were goods, or if it were services; applying it to a real mixed invoice overstates the total tax due.

Why is only 20% of the tax withheld, not the whole amount?

The general sales-tax-withholding rule (used by FBR and every provincial authority) has the paying agent withhold only 1/5th (20%) of the tax shown on the invoice when the supplier is registered and active on the Active Taxpayer List — the supplier remits the remaining 4/5th itself through its own monthly return. The full 100% is withheld only when the supplier is unregistered or ATL-inactive.

Does this tool include income tax withholding too?

No — this calculator covers sales tax (GST and provincial) only. Income tax withholding under Section 153 of the Income Tax Ordinance is deducted from the same payment separately; use the Withholding Tax (WHT) calculator to work that out and combine it with the sales tax figures here for the full picture.

What if my category isn't a standard 20%/100% withholding case?

A handful of categories deviate from the general rule — advertisement services are withheld in full regardless of registration, federal wholesalers/dealers get a reduced 1/10th rate, and Punjab's telecom/banking/insurance sector is withheld at 80%. For those, use the Sales Tax Withholding calculator, which models each category individually.
